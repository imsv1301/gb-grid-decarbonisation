When is the grid cleanest?

Average carbon intensity for each hour of the day and month of the year, over the latest 12 months of data (UK local time).

From October to February, electricity is cleanest around $w_clean overnight and dirtiest around $w_dirty, at the evening demand peak. From April to August, solar moves the cleanest window to the middle of the day, around $s_clean, and the dirtiest to around $s_dirty. Running an EV charger or a heat pump in the clean window cuts its emissions without using any less power.

How the data was checked

The source has $rows half-hourly rows. Before any analysis, the pipeline runs SQL checks and stops if the basics fail.

  • No duplicate timestamps and no missing half-hours between the first and last reading.
  • The individual fuel columns add up to the published total generation in every row.
  • The published zero-carbon percentage column disagrees with its own zero-carbon total in $mismatch rows ($mismatch_pct%), and goes above 100% in $over100. This analysis ignores the published percentage columns and recalculates every share from the raw megawatt figures.
  • $coal_blips half-hours report small amounts of coal after Britain's last coal station closed. They are too small to affect any result and are kept as published.

Method and code

Python and pandas load and tidy the data, SQL run in DuckDB does the aggregation, and Plotly draws the charts. Averages are weighted by generation, so a windy night with low demand doesn't count as much as a busy weekday. A scheduled GitHub Actions workflow downloads the latest data and rebuilds this page every week.

Code, SQL and processed data on GitHub. Source: NESO historic generation mix, published under the NESO Open Data Licence.
