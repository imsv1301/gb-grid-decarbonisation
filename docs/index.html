Britain's electricity is 72% cleaner than in 2009

Each stripe below is one month of the GB grid, coloured by how many grams of CO₂ were emitted for every kilowatt-hour generated. In 2009 the average was 459 g/kWh. In 2025 it was 130.

Coal went, wind arrived

The dirtiest year on record was 2012, when coal supplied 42% of generation and the grid averaged 510 g/kWh. Coal then fell from 24% in 2015 to 9% in 2016, with gas (42%) filling most of the gap. The last half-hour where coal made up more than 1% of generation was 30 Sep 2024, 15:00.

Wind generated more than gas across a full year for the first time in 2024. In 2025 wind supplied 30% of generation against 27% for gas, and imports reached 15%.

When is the grid cleanest?

Average carbon intensity for each hour of the day and month of the year, over the latest 12 months of data (UK local time).

From October to February, electricity is cleanest around 04:00 overnight and dirtiest around 17:00, at the evening demand peak. From April to August, solar moves the cleanest window to the middle of the day, around 14:00, and the dirtiest to around 21:00. Running an EV charger or a heat pump in the clean window cuts its emissions without using any less power.

How the data was checked

The source has 311,353 half-hourly rows. Before any analysis, the pipeline runs SQL checks and stops if the basics fail.

  • No duplicate timestamps and no missing half-hours between the first and last reading.
  • The individual fuel columns add up to the published total generation in every row.
  • The published zero-carbon percentage column disagrees with its own zero-carbon total in 267,550 rows (86%), and goes above 100% in 2. This analysis ignores the published percentage columns and recalculates every share from the raw megawatt figures.
  • 24 half-hours report small amounts of coal after Britain's last coal station closed. They are too small to affect any result and are kept as published.

Method and code

Python and pandas load and tidy the data, SQL run in DuckDB does the aggregation, and Plotly draws the charts. Averages are weighted by generation, so a windy night with low demand doesn't count as much as a busy weekday. A scheduled GitHub Actions workflow downloads the latest data and rebuilds this page every week.

Code, SQL and processed data on GitHub. Source: NESO historic generation mix, published under the NESO Open Data Licence.
